D-BUS
=====

CATEGORY: message bus and peer-to-peer IPC protocol; the Linux desktop standard
LICENSE: *** CAUTION. *** libdbus is dual GPLv2+ OR AFL-2.1. Neither choice is
  comfortable: GPL is strong copyleft, and the AFL patent clause has a long-
  disputed interaction with LGPL linking (dbus and gtk-devel list threads, 2007
  and 2009). GDBus, in GLib, is LGPL — weaker, still copyleft for static
  linking, which is how this project ships C++ (/MT). sd-bus in systemd is
  LGPL-2.1 (UNVERIFIED by us; check before relying on it) and is Linux-only in
  practice.
  There is no permissive implementation of the reference stack. That alone is
  close to disqualifying for a library we intend adopters to CARRY.
ADDED: 2026-09-05 — IPC survey for the channel layer (SCOPE.md §5).

WHAT IT IS
  A protocol plus, usually, a broker. The base protocol is one-to-one; the
  dbus-daemon adds routing among many applications, names, and activation.

THE DAEMON IS NOT ACTUALLY MANDATORY
  This is the fact most often got wrong, including by an earlier draft of ours.
  libdbus provides peer-to-peer connections for direct one-to-one messaging, and
  peer-to-peer mode works on Windows and macOS. Developers targeting those
  platforms commonly use it precisely to avoid distributing and managing a
  background service.
  So D-Bus passes the carry test on paper. It fails on licence, and on the
  amount of Linux-desktop assumption that comes with the ecosystem rather than
  the protocol.

CALLER IDENTITY
  The strongest ecosystem answer of anything surveyed, and the source of the
  problem we care about: D-Bus historically identified peers by the pid from
  SO_PEERCRED, which is racy. SO_PEERPIDFD was proposed by a Wayland developer
  explicitly to fix that (see research/integration/caller-identity.txt).
  Its conclusion is the finding we are designing against: even with a race-free
  pid, there is no standardized way to learn WHAT the process is, so the answer
  became per-application sockets with metadata attached at creation.

KNOWN LIMITS WORTH RECORDING
  - Multiplexing through the broker forces all messages into one process, which
    makes per-connection handling awkward.
  - No flow control; documented as unusable for streaming or many large objects.
    Our download layer is exactly "many large objects", so this is not academic.
  - The broker cannot start until filesystems are mounted, which is why systemd
    wanted something usable earlier in boot.
